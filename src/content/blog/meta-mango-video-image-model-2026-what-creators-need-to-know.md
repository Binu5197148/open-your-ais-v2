<p><em>Updated November 6, 2026. This piece was written in March, when "Mango" was only a reported codename with a first half of 2026 target (<a href="https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/19/meta-is-developing-a-new-image-and-video-model-for-a-2026-release-report-says/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">TechCrunch</a>). On July 7, 2026, Meta launched Muse Image and showed an early preview of Muse Video, its first video model from Meta Superintelligence Labs (<a href="https://ai.meta.com/blog/introducing-muse-image-muse-video-msl/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Meta announcement</a>). Meta's post does not use the name Mango, so linking the two is an inference from timing and scope. That post said Muse Video was coming to creators and Meta AI. On September 15, Meta's <a href="https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-meta-one-subscription-service-more-features-ai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Meta One announcement</a> listed generating videos in Meta AI, powered by Muse models, with plans and benefits that vary by region, app and account. It does not name Muse Video, so I cannot tell you which model answers in your account, and I found no public Muse Video API from Meta as of today. This is an analysis of public documents, not a hands-on test.</em></p>

<h2>What Did Meta Say Was Weak in Muse Video?</h2>
<p>In the July preview, the company named two weak spots it was still working on: audio and video synchronization, and physically accurate fast motion. That is a July snapshot, not a November test, and I found no newer statement from Meta on either one. They are not small gaps. Dialogue and action are where a generated clip stops being a demo and starts being a scene.</p>
<p>The preview ranked third for text to video on Arena's human preference chart, by Meta's own account in July. A high preview ranking and a tool you can schedule a job around are different things. Native audio sounds great until the lips drift.</p>
<p>Meta's October news was not video either. Muse Wonder, announced October 12, is an agent model for multi step tasks (<a href="https://es.euronews.com/2026/10/13/meta-lanza-muse-wonder-sus-modelos-de-ia-mas-potentes-hasta-ahora" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Euronews</a>, <a href="https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/meta-releases-wonder-opens-spark/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Batch</a>). If someone tells you Meta shipped its big model and you plan to make video with it, check which Muse they mean.</p>

<h2>How Do You Use the Meta AI Video Generator Today?</h2>
<p>Three questions get mixed up here, and each has its own answer.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Can you make video in Meta AI?</strong> Meta says so. The <a href="https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-meta-one-subscription-service-more-features-ai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Meta One announcement</a> lists video generation with Muse models, says Meta AI stays free for everyday use, and sells more generation in paid bundles such as Core, at $7.99 a month. Plans are rolling out gradually, so the answer depends on your region, app and account.</li>
<li><strong>Which model is it?</strong> Meta does not say. The post names Muse models in general, not Muse Video.</li>
<li><strong>Can you build on it?</strong> I found no public Muse Video API from Meta as of November 6. Third party services advertise routes, but those are not Meta endpoints.</li>
</ul>
<p>The route with the most public documentation is Vibes. It launched in September 2025 inside the Meta AI app and on meta.ai, and Meta's launch partners for it were Midjourney and Black Forest Labs (<a href="https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/meta-launches-vibes-a-new-way-of-creating-and-remixing-ai-videos" target="_blank" rel="noopener">TechRadar</a>). The flow is simple:</p>
<ol>
<li>Open the Meta AI app or meta.ai and go to Vibes.</li>
<li>Generate a clip from a prompt, or start from a video already in the feed and remix it.</li>
<li>Cross post to Instagram or Facebook Stories and Reels, or send it by message.</li>
</ol>
<p>In February 2026, Meta confirmed it was testing a separate Vibes app, first in Brazil and Mexico (<a href="https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/metas-testing-a-standalone-ai-video-app/811380/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Social Media Today</a>, <a href="https://www.pymnts.com/meta/2026/meta-challenges-sora-with-standalone-vibes-app/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PYMNTS</a>). I could not confirm where that test stands now, so check your local app store before you plan around it.</p>
<p>The image side is further along. Muse Image is live in the Meta AI app, on meta.ai, in Instagram Stories in the US, and in WhatsApp in limited countries, and it carries Content Seal, an invisible watermark. In July, Meta said it plans to extend that to video, and I could not confirm that it has.</p>
<pre><code>Before you put Meta AI video in a client plan

1. Do not write "Muse Video" in a plan unless Meta names it for your tool.
2. Test it in your country and account, today.
3. Never promise dialogue sync. Meta listed it as weak in July.
4. Test fast action in your own account before you promise it.
5. Check the provenance label on exports before delivery.
6. Get written consent for any real face or real account.
7. Keep a second tool ready for the same shot.</code></pre>

<h2>The Consent Lesson From Muse Image</h2>
<p>Three days after launch, on July 10, Meta pulled a Muse Image feature that let people tag a public Instagram account and generate images from its photos. Public accounts were opted in by default, and the feature went down after backlash from users and talent representatives (<a href="https://engadget.com/2212843/meta-deactivates-muse-image-public-instagram-post-ai-images" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Engadget</a>, <a href="https://the-decoder.com/meta-kills-muse-image-feature-that-let-anyone-generate-ai-photos-of-instagram-users-without-consent/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Decoder</a>). Muse Image itself stayed up.</p>
<p>Distribution is Meta's advantage, as the rest of this piece argues. That same advantage means a bad default reaches everyone at once. Anyone building client work on these tools should treat consent as part of the brief, not as something the platform handles for you.</p>

<p><em>The next sections are the March read, kept as a record of what was expected. Where they say Mango or the first half of 2026, they describe the roadmap as reported then, not today's product.</em></p>

<h2>Meta Building Its Own Video Model Is a Different Kind of News</h2>
<p>When Kling, Seedance, or Google release a new model, creators who know those tools notice. When Meta releases a model, it affects everyone on Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and whatever mixed reality platform they are building toward. The scale of distribution is categorically different.</p>
<p>In March, Meta was reported to be developing an image and video AI model internally codenamed "Mango," alongside a text model called "Avocado." The information came from a roadmap presentation by Alexandr Wang (formerly of Scale AI, now at Meta) and Meta's Chief Product Officer, Chris Cox. The planned release window was the first half of 2026.</p>
<p>Mango was described as capable of text-to-video, video-to-video, and fine-grained editing in addition to high-fidelity image generation.</p>

<h2>What the Capabilities Mean in Practice</h2>
<p>Text-to-video from Meta means generating video clips through the same interface where you already manage your Instagram and Facebook presence. No external tool. No file export. No API integration. You prompt, you generate, you post, or you generate directly as part of the content creation flow within Meta's apps.</p>
<p>Video-to-video means you can take existing footage and transform it (change style, alter lighting, replace elements, extend or modify content) using AI within the platform. For brands and creators who produce large volumes of social content, the ability to repurpose and adapt existing footage without a production cycle has real value.</p>
<p>Fine-grained editing, adjusting specific elements of a generated image or video without regenerating the whole piece, is the capability that matters most for professional use. Being able to say "change the background color" or "adjust the lighting on this subject" and have the system execute precisely is what separates a production tool from a generation toy.</p>

<h2>Why Meta's Ecosystem Position Changes Everything</h2>
<p>Seedance has multi scene consistency. Kling has Motion Control. Gemini Omni can fly a camera through almost anything. But none of them have three billion users and built-in distribution to Instagram Reels, Facebook Feed, WhatsApp Status, and Meta's XR platform.</p>
<p>When Meta ships Mango natively into its apps, AI-generated video becomes available to every creator and brand on those platforms without any technical barrier. No account on a separate tool. No API access. No learning curve beyond what already exists in the apps.</p>
<p>This changes the competitive question for independent AI video tools. Their moat is currently quality and control. Meta's moat will be distribution and integration. The question for the next 18 months is whether quality remains differentiated enough that professional creators pay for specialized tools, or whether Meta's "good enough" with built-in distribution captures the majority of use cases.</p>

<h2>What This Means for Brands and Commercial Creators</h2>
<p>For anyone producing commercial social content (brand campaigns, product launches, always-on content for Instagram and Facebook), the implications are worth thinking through now rather than after the launch.</p>
<p>If Mango delivers on its described capabilities, the cost of producing social video content drops further. A brand that currently outsources social content production to agencies or independent creators gains the ability to generate content natively in the platform where it will be distributed.</p>
<p>That is not necessarily bad for skilled creators. It creates a floor: anyone can generate generic content. It raises the premium on content that demonstrates clear creative direction, specific visual identity, and authentic brand voice. The commodity work gets automated. The work that requires genuine creative expertise becomes more valuable by contrast.</p>
<p>The practical response for independent creators and small production companies: define your creative differentiation clearly now, before the tools that commoditize generic content are in everyone's hands. What you make should be clearly yours, not just technically proficient.</p>

<h2>The Timeline, Revisited</h2>
<p>In March the planned window was the first half of 2026, and this piece warned that availability for creators might slide into the second half. It did. Images and a video preview arrived in July, and by September Meta was selling video generation inside Meta AI without saying which model runs it.</p>
<p>The useful test now is the one you run in your own account, to find the quality ceiling and how it fits a workflow. The gap between announced capability and usability in production is where the useful evaluation happens.</p>

<h2>What to prepare in advance</h2>
<p>There is no reason to wait for a clearer answer from Meta to start preparing. The shift is already happening, unevenly, across the social platforms. A brand feed now has to look distinct from the generic AI output everyone has access to.</p>
<p>The practical move is to lock down a visual identity that survives the homogenization wave. A specific color palette. A consistent crop and pacing. A typographic treatment that does not look generated. None of this is hard. It just requires choosing, and most brands have not chosen, because the volume of content production was always urgent enough that taste decisions got deferred.</p>
<p>As generated video spreads through these apps, the brands without a visible identity will look like everyone else. The ones that picked their look on purpose will stand out by default.</p>
