<p>Higgsfield Editing Room went live on November 20, 2026. It is a video-to-video editor for footage you already have. You upload a clip of up to 10 minutes, mark a moment, and change who is in it, where the camera was, where the scene happens, how the camera moves or how it is lit.</p>

<p>On a commercial set there is one sentence everybody learns to distrust: "we fix it in post." Editing Room is that sentence turned into a product with six buttons.</p>

<p>This is a read of Higgsfield's changelog entry and of the Editing Room page itself, which I opened on November 23, 2026. I have not run a job through it, so nothing below is a result of mine. Higgsfield is the hub I work in, which is why I read its release notes when they come out. When I looked, I found no independent review of the tool yet. Every fact here is Higgsfield's own statement, linked at the end.</p>

<h2>What is Higgsfield Editing Room?</h2>

<p>The <a href="https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/changelog" target="_blank" rel="noopener">changelog</a> describes it as an editor where you "upload a clip, pick any moment, and swap the person, angle, location, camera move, or lighting in one place." Two details in that entry matter more than the list of tools.</p>

<p>The first: edits apply only to the moment you selected. The rest of the video stays as it was. The second: edits stack. In Higgsfield's example, a new angle can get a camera move and then new lighting without uploading the file again.</p>

<p>It is not a generator. Most of what Higgsfield sells starts from a prompt or a still, the way <a href="/blog/higgsfield-cinema-studio-4-emotion-wheel-director-read/">Cinema Studio</a> does. This starts from your footage, shot on a camera or made in another tool.</p>

<p>It is also not Miracle. <a href="https://higgsfield.ai/miracle" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Miracle</a> is a separate Higgsfield product for editing a clip while it plays, and its page still says "coming soon" with a waitlist. Editing Room is the one you can open today.</p>

<h2>What does each tool do, and for how long?</h2>

<p>The page lists six tools. Each one comes with two labels that are easy to miss: whether it works across cuts or inside one cut, and how long the moment can be.</p>

<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Tool</th><th>What the page says it does</th><th>Scope</th><th>Length</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Swap person</td><td>"Put someone else in the shot." Replaces a person, an object or the whole style in the part you pick</td><td>Across cuts</td><td>2 to 30s</td></tr>
<tr><td>Change angle</td><td>Shows the same moment from another side, "as if a second camera had been there"</td><td>Cut only</td><td>2 to 15s</td></tr>
<tr><td>Change location</td><td>Moves the shot to a new place</td><td>Across cuts</td><td>2 to 30s</td></tr>
<tr><td>Move camera</td><td>Adds a dolly, a pan or an orbit to a shot that was filmed standing still</td><td>Cut only</td><td>2 to 15s</td></tr>
<tr><td>Relight</td><td>Changes the time of day, the colour and where the light comes from</td><td>Cut only</td><td>2 to 15s</td></tr>
<tr><td>Edit with prompt</td><td>You paint over one thing and say what it should become. Only that area changes</td><td>Cut only</td><td>2 to 30s</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<p>Read the two columns on the right before anything else. You can upload 10 minutes, but every edit lives inside 2 to 15 seconds, or 2 to 30 for the swaps and the brush. I read "Cut only" as an edit that stays inside a single shot, and "Across cuts" as one that can carry over several. That is my reading of the label, not a line from the documentation.</p>

<p>One more limit, shown when the page is opened on a phone: Editing Room is desktop only for now.</p>

<h2>How do you use Higgsfield Editing Room?</h2>

<p>The page has a three-step panel and the changelog fills in the rest. Put together, the sequence is this:</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>Upload the video on a desktop browser.</strong> The tool splits it into clips automatically.</li>
<li><strong>Correct the cuts.</strong> "Cut here" splits a shot where the automatic cut missed. Then mark a moment on the lane above the clip and adjust its edges.</li>
<li><strong>Choose one tool for that moment.</strong> For Relight, the changelog lists presets, custom light sources with direction, color, brightness and diffusion, or a text description.</li>
<li><strong>Stack the next effect on the same moment</strong> if the shot needs it. The panel on the page shows a Relight, a Move camera and a <a href="/blog/higgsfield-genjutsu-motion-transfer-object-swap-director-test/">Genjutsu</a> effect in the same queue.</li>
<li><strong>Let them run and compare.</strong> Several edits can run at once. Each one can be checked against the original in Split or Compare view.</li>
<li><strong>Export</strong> the finished video.</li>
</ol>

<p>If you came here asking how to use AI for video editing in general, this is the shape most of these tools are converging on: your footage in, one marked moment, one named change, a before and after.</p>

<h2>The six buttons are six jobs</h2>

<p>Look at the list again and put a call sheet next to it. Change angle is the second camera operator. Move camera is the dolly grip. Relight is the gaffer. Change location is the scout and the art department. Swap person is casting. The brush is props.</p>

<p>The common reading of a release like this is that those jobs just got cheaper. I read it the other way. On a set, none of those people only executes. The gaffer asks what the scene is about before a light goes up. The camera operator tells you the second angle will not cut with the first. They argue with the director before the shot exists, and the film is better for the argument.</p>

<p>A tool that relights after the fact answers a smaller question: what can still be saved. That is a useful question. Every job has the take where the performance was right and the light was not, or the product shot that needed one slow push in and got a locked frame. For that shot, six buttons beat a reshoot.</p>

<p>What the tool cannot do is know which light is wrong. Somebody still has to look at the frame and say it in set language. The limits in the table say the same thing in numbers: 15 seconds of new light is a rescue, not a lighting plan.</p>

<p>So my rule is plain. Shoot it properly, and keep this for the shot that got away and for the version the client asks for after the fact.</p>

<h2>The sheet to fill before you upload</h2>

<p>The fastest way to waste generations is to open the tool and start clicking. Write one of these per moment first. It takes two minutes and it is the brief the tool does not ask you for.</p>

```text
EDITING ROOM SHEET (one per moment)

Clip:            [file name, total length]
Moment:          [in 00:00, out 00:00] (2 to 15s, or 2 to 30s for swaps and brush)
What is wrong:   [one sentence in set language: flat light, dead frame, wrong wall]
Tool:            [Swap person / Change angle / Change location / Move camera / Relight / Brush]
Instruction:     [what it should become. One change only]
Must not change: [face, product, logo, wardrobe, screen direction]
Stack order:     [1st angle, 2nd camera move, 3rd light]
Rights:          [who is on screen, and do I have permission for this change]
Judge:           [who compares before and after, against which reference]
```

<p>"What is wrong" comes before "Tool" on purpose. If you cannot name the problem, picking a button is guessing.</p>

<p>The Rights line is not decoration. Swap person puts a different human being in footage somebody else performed in. Read the contract of the performer you are replacing, and get permission in writing from the person whose face goes in. The tool will not ask.</p>

<h2>What I would check before trusting it on a job</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The price.</strong> The page shows no cost before you upload, and I did not run an edit. The demo tags its seven example edits as "Free generation". I do not know what that covers, so I am not calling the tool free. Look at what it charges before the first Generate, and if you drive Higgsfield from Claude, keep <a href="/blog/higgsfield-mcp-claude-setup-credits-director-rules/">the credit rules</a> close.</li>
<li><strong>Continuity across the limit.</strong> A 40-second take with dialogue is three moments. Three separate relights have to match each other, and nothing on the page promises they will.</li>
<li><strong>Faces and logos.</strong> Any tool that redraws a frame can drift on the two things a client checks first. Compare view exists for this. Use it at full size.</li>
<li><strong>The export.</strong> Neither the changelog nor the page states an output resolution or codec. Test with one short clip before you promise a delivery format.</li>
</ul>

<p>"We fix it in post" was always a promise somebody else had to keep. Now there is a tool that keeps part of it. The part it leaves out is the one that was never technical: deciding, before the camera rolls, what the shot is for.</p>
